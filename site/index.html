One conversational turn

Every message is a small act of organization.

There are no modes to switch between — no “write” here and “ask” there. Recording and answering happen in the same breath.

  1. 01

    You say it plainly

    Type the way you'd tell a colleague. Sediment snapshots the whole formation first, so any change is reversible from the very next second.

  2. 02

    It grounds before it acts

    The people and things you named are resolved to their notes and current facts. A Working Set of what's in play is handed to the agent — so it never starts a turn guessing.

  3. 03

    It records — and pushes back

    Facts are written into your notes and the knowledge graph. If something is vague or collides with what it already knows, the agent asks before committing.

  4. 04

    Everything is revertable

    The audit log lists every turn's changes. Undo a whole turn or a single fact, with a quiet ten-second undo for the small stuff.

A bi-temporal knowledge graph

It knows when things were true.

Underneath the notes is a graph of facts — relationships between people, organizations, and projects, each carrying a validity window. When a fact changes, the old edge keeps its history; when a fact was simply wrong, it's retracted as if it never was.

That's what lets the agent notice a contradiction instead of silently overwriting you — and what makes “what did I think last spring?” a question with an answer.

Knowledge isn't filed. It settles.

A single conversation rarely lands a finished thought. It deposits a layer — a fact here, a question there, a loop left open. Over weeks, those layers compress into something with structure and history you can read back: strata. Sediment is built for that slow accretion, not for a tidy inbox you'll never reach the bottom of.
